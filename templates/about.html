Our Story

Rooted in a City
Built on Wood Carving

Saharanpur has carried the title of India's wood-carving capital for generations — a city where entire neighborhoods have practiced hand-chiseled woodwork since the 19th century, exporting furniture across the world. WoodX Interior, registered as Wood Ex Interior & Co., is a workshop built inside that same tradition.

We don't outsource our carving or run it through a CNC machine. Every headboard, every sofa frame, every dining table leg is chiselled by hand by karigars who learned the craft the way Saharanpur has always taught it — from a senior craftsman standing beside them. What comes out of our workshop is furniture with the same soul as the carved havelis this city is known for, just built for a modern home.

What started as a small family workshop has grown, order by order and referral by referral, into a name homeowners, interior designers, and hoteliers across North India now call directly when they want something built properly. We've never chased volume over quality — we'd rather turn away a rushed order than send out a piece we wouldn't put in our own home.

Our Timber Philosophy

Why We're Picky
About Wood

Most of the furniture that warps, cracks, or comes apart within a couple of years wasn't badly designed — it was built from timber that was never properly seasoned. That's the corner we refuse to cut. Every plank of Teak (Sagwan), Sheesham, Oak, or Walnut that enters our Saharanpur workshop is kiln-dried until its moisture content is low enough to survive North Indian summers and monsoons without shifting.

Sheesham, in particular, is a wood our region knows intimately — it's dense, holds fine carving detail better than almost anything else, and darkens beautifully with age. Teak is our go-to when a client wants something termite-resistant with a warm, honey-toned grain. We don't believe in a single "best" wood; we believe in matching the right timber to how a piece will actually be used in your home.

This is also why we insist on hand-carving over machine routing for anything with detail. A CNC bit cuts a clean, repeatable groove — but it cuts every piece exactly the same, and it can't adjust for the grain of the specific plank in front of it the way a karigar's hand can. That adaptability is the difference between furniture that looks carved and furniture that actually is.
